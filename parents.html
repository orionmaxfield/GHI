You don’t have to figure this out alone tonight.

Maybe your child came to you recently. Maybe you found out another way. Either way, you’re probably feeling pressure to act quickly, drowning in conflicting information, and wondering whether wanting to slow down makes you a bad parent.

It doesn’t. Your instinct to pause is not a failure of love.

What we can do for you

Find a therapist who fits. We maintain a network of independent licensed therapists across the country who work with families like yours by telehealth — clinicians who will make room for your child’s experience and for your values at the same time.

Parent coaching while you wait, or alongside. Our intake coordinator offers educational coaching: help articulating your own values clearly, and practical ways to keep the relationship with your child strong through hard conversations. This is not therapy, and we’re careful about that line — when what’s needed is clinical care, we say so and help you find it.

A free fifteen-minute conversation with our intake coordinator, before anything else, to talk through your situation and what options actually exist.

Clarity, then connection

The approach we look for in therapists, and teach in coaching, has a simple order. First you get clear on your own values — what you believe, what you hope for, what you can and can’t carry. Only then do you work on the bridge: staying in real relationship with a child who may see things differently. Skipping the first step is why so many of these conversations collapse.

We’re building a written guide that walks parents through this, step by step, for the first weeks. If you’d like it when it’s ready, mention it in your email.

Common worries, answered honestly

“Will you take my side?” We won’t take political sides — that’s a promise we make to every family, and it’s what makes this work possible. What we will do is take your family’s side: connection and clarity over conflict.

“How fast can this happen?” Honestly: matching can take time. The right therapist for this is rare, and we’d rather be slow and right than fast and wrong.

“What will it cost?” Fees are set by each therapist and insurance varies; some families pay out of pocket. We’ll be upfront about this early.
